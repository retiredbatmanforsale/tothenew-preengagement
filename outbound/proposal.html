Teach the org to ship with AI& prove it moved the needle.

A two-dimensional engagement, in two coupled deliverables. The first upskills your engineers on AI-powered development in 25 hands-on hours. The second is the observability engine that makes the productivity gain legible to leadership in numbers, every day after.

01 · Problem

The pressure is specific — and so is the gap.

Every engineering leader at a services firm is currently being squeezed on the same question: where are the AI productivity gains, and can you prove them? Clients won't accept "we're investing in it." Boards won't accept anecdote. Competitors who can quantify a delivery-velocity step-change have an asymmetric story in every procurement conversation.

The market response so far has mostly been one-off internal trainings — half-day workshops, recorded webinars, a few licenses for engineers who already liked tinkering. These leave no measurable trail and decay fast. What's missing isn't more training; it's training that's tightly coupled to the dashboard that will be used to judge whether it worked.

This proposal is the engagement just shipped at ToTheNew, generalized for [Client]. The shape is the same; the content adapts to your stack, cohort, and client mix.

02 · Approach

Two dimensions, deliberately coupled.

Training without measurement decays. Measurement without training shows nothing. The engagement runs both halves on a single timeline, so the metrics that prove the training worked are the same metrics the team learns to influence.

AI Learning Curriculum

10 days × 2.5 hours, hands-on, Claude Code + Cursor. Zero-to-hero arc designed for engineers who already use Copilot casually and need to become productive with agentic workflows on real brownfield codebases.

Consumer · Engineering cohort

Observability & Adoption Engine

24×7 dashboard against four categories — Activity, Throughput, Adoption, Quality — that defends the training to leadership. Every metric is grounded in industry practice (DORA, GitHub/Anthropic enterprise admin telemetry).

Consumer · Engineering leadership

04 · Dimension 2

Observability scaffold — four categories, grounded in industry practice.

24 metrics across four categories, each instrumented from an established source (Git host, JIRA, IDE telemetry, Anthropic billing). The scaffold is intentionally redundant — every velocity number pairs with a quality counterweight, so leadership can't be sold speed at the cost of stability.

Activity

The raw volume signals. Easy to game alone; valuable as denominators for the categories below.

  • LOC / dev / year — the perf-review datapoint at Google, Meta, Amazon
  • PRs raised, CLs reviewed, design docs reviewed
  • Time spent coding · agent vs user hours
Throughput

"Did we get faster" — every line here pairs with a quality line. The DORA metrics anchor this category.

  • Ticket cycle time · DORA p50 — the headline metric
  • SWE hours saved, features shipped per sprint
  • Brownfield velocity, plan-first ratio
Adoption

Are tools actually being used, with the configurations that make them productive? Decay shows here first.

  • Cursor DAU, Claude Code DAU
  • CLAUDE.md coverage — leading indicator of durable productivity
  • Token consumption, custom commands, MCP server usage
Quality

The counterweight to throughput. If speed celebrates while quality silently drops, the engagement did damage, not value.

  • PR rework rate, post-merge bug rate · DORA change-failure
  • AI-touch ratio on bugs · the "is AI making us worse?" answer
  • Test coverage on AI code, hallucination incidents, context-grounded prompt ratio
05 · Model

Engagement model — timeline, deliverables, your part.

Timeline

Week 0 · Baseline

Pre-engagement survey, license confirmation, repo access, two weeks of pre-training measurement so post-training has something to compare against.

Weeks 1–2 · Delivery

The 10-day curriculum, offline, 2.5 hrs/day. Real TTN-style tickets sourced from your codebase for Days 6, 8, 10.

Weeks 3+ · Sustain

Dashboard goes live. Bi-weekly metric review for the first quarter. Curriculum artifacts (CLAUDE.md, custom commands) maintained in your repos by your team.

Deliverables

  • Cohort onboarding survey + baseline report
  • 10 days of facilitated training (in-person)
  • Per-attendee CLAUDE.md + .cursorrules committed to at least one of your real repos
  • Per-attendee shipped PR (Day 10 capstone)
  • Observability dashboard wired against your Git host, JIRA, IDE telemetry, Anthropic billing
  • Metric documentation + 1-pager for leadership
  • 90-day post-engagement check-in

What [Client] provides

  • Cohort of engineers, blocked on calendars for the 10 days
  • Cursor + Claude Code licenses for the cohort — during AND for 90+ days after
  • Access to representative codebases for Days 6, 8, 9
  • Real tickets from your backlog for Days 6, 8, 10
  • Engineering sponsor empowered to commit CLAUDE.md to those repos
  • API access to Git host, JIRA, IDE admin for instrumentation
  • Two-week pre-engagement window for baseline measurement
06 · Open

Open questions before we lock the plan.

Decisions that, once answered, move this from a proposal into an executable engagement. None block design; all block delivery.

  1. Cohort size, composition, and split.One shared track works to ~30 engineers per cohort. Above that, parallel sessions. Stack mix decides whether Day 8 forks four ways or fewer.
  2. License coverage during and after.If engineers fall back to free tiers after Week 2, the dashboard numbers will flatten and the engagement will look like it failed.
  3. Real-problem sourcing.One unfamiliar repo for Day 6, four small role-specific tasks for Day 8, one real ticket per attendee for Day 10. Toy problems lose the room.
  4. Baseline measurement window.Two weeks of pre-training instrumentation so the after-picture has something to compare against. Earlier the better.
  5. Metric mix calibration.What [Client] already tracks vs. what we'd add. The dashboard inherits your existing trust where possible; nothing duplicated.
"The training has to teach the right skills, and the dashboard has to measure the right things — or neither dimension does its job."
